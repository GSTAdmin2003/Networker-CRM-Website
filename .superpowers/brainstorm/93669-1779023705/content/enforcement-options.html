How strict do you want enforcement?

Three levels of strictness — pick how much control you want.

A

Perceptual Balance

Tighten padding on dark sections at mobile breakpoints so they don't balloon. Simple CSS-only. The ratio stays close but isn't mathematically enforced — just feels right at every size.

padding: 56px → 40px at mobile on .problem and .waitlist

B

Viewport-Anchored Heights

Give dark sections a min-height in svh units (e.g. min-height: 28svh) so their height is proportional to the viewport at every size — not just padded. More precise than A.

Still CSS-only. Requires some tuning per section. Ratio holds loosely at all sizes.

C

JS-Enforced Ratio

A ResizeObserver measures actual rendered section heights on every resize, calculates the ratio, and adjusts --section-padding CSS custom properties live to bring it back to 60·30·10.

Mathematically accurate. Adds JS overhead. Overkill for a landing page.

My recommendation: Option A — perceptual balance via tighter mobile padding. The 6·3·1 rule is a design guideline, not an engineering constraint. Close enough at every viewport is the right target.
